# MASTER BUILD PROMPT — PrintShop SaaS
## Complete Shopkeeper Dashboard: Zero to Production-Ready Implementation

### 1. Your role and mission

Act as a senior full-stack engineer, SaaS architect, database engineer, UI/UX designer, security engineer, and QA specialist.

Your task is to inspect, improve, and complete my existing PrintShop SaaS application by implementing a **fully functional, professional, mobile-first shopkeeper dashboard**.

The application is already deployed on Vercel and connected to Supabase. The project is actively being developed, and the infrastructure may be upgraded in the future.

**Do not rebuild the application from scratch. Do not replace the existing infrastructure. Inspect the codebase, preserve working functionality, and complete the missing features.**

The primary goal is to allow an ordinary printing-shop owner in India to independently set up and operate their business through the dashboard without needing the platform administrator to manually configure the shop.

The shopkeeper should be able to:

1. Register and access their account.
2. Create and configure their shop.
3. Add business details, address, contact information, and opening hours.
4. Configure printing services and prices.
5. Upload their existing UPI payment QR image.
6. Configure their UPI ID and payment contact details.
7. Generate and download a unique QR code for their shop's ordering page.
8. Receive customer orders with unique token numbers.
9. See orders and payment statuses in the dashboard.
10. Verify direct UPI payments or receive verified payment updates through an existing supported integration.
11. Manage printing, pickup, cancellations, and completed orders.
12. Manage staff, notifications, shop settings, and usage.
13. Operate the shop without accessing another shop's information.

Build actual working functionality, not mockups, placeholder forms, fabricated analytics, or buttons that do nothing.

# 3. Product architecture and scope

Build the shopkeeper experience as part of the existing application.

The overall product has three connected areas:

1. **Shopkeeper dashboard:** the main scope of this task.
2. **Customer-facing shop page and ordering flow:** implement the necessary integration and existing capabilities; prepare the complete public experience if it is missing, following the phased priorities below.
3. **Platform administrator dashboard:** reuse the existing administrator functionality and make only the changes necessary for shop onboarding, account status, and support.

The shopkeeper dashboard must be usable independently of unfinished future features.

Do not spend the entire implementation building a new storefront before the shopkeeper can configure the business and manage orders.

## Critical QR distinction

There are two separate QR codes.

**A. Shop-ordering QR**

- Unique to each shop.
- Opens that shop's public ordering page.
- Used by customers to browse shop details, services, prices, and place orders.
- Generated by this platform.
- Downloadable and printable by the shopkeeper.

**B. UPI payment QR**

- Belongs to the shopkeeper's payment account.
- The shopkeeper can upload an existing QR image supplied by their bank or UPI app.
- Used by customers to make payments directly to the shopkeeper.
- Must not be confused with the shop-ordering QR.

The two QR codes must have separate settings, previews, labels, storage references, and dashboard actions.

Never substitute one QR code for the other.

# 13. Payment verification workflow

This is one of the most important parts of the application.

Payment status and order status must be independent.

## Payment statuses

Support the existing equivalents of:

- Pending.
- Awaiting Verification.
- Paid.
- Failed.
- Cancelled.
- Refund Pending.
- Refunded.
- Partially Refunded, if supported.

## Direct UPI workflow

When customers pay directly to the shopkeeper's UPI account:

1. The customer sees the shop's approved UPI QR or UPI ID.
2. The customer makes the payment using their UPI app.
3. The customer can submit a transaction reference if the workflow requires it.
4. The order is shown in the dashboard with the correct token.
5. The payment remains pending or awaiting verification until confirmed.
6. The shopkeeper opens the payment details.
7. The shopkeeper checks the actual receipt using their available bank or UPI records.
8. The shopkeeper confirms or rejects the claim.
9. The system records the result and audit details.
10. The dashboard reflects the verified state after the database transaction succeeds.

Customer-submitted screenshots and transaction IDs are claims, not independent proof of successful payment.

## Payment verification actions

Provide authorized actions such as:

- Confirm payment received.
- Reject payment claim.
- Record transaction reference.
- Record verified amount.
- Add internal verification note.
- View payment history.
- Correct an earlier verification with a required reason and audit entry, where permitted.

Confirmation must require explicit user action.

Before marking a payment paid, validate that:

- The order belongs to the current shop.
- The actor has payment-verification permission.
- The amount is valid.
- The currency is correct.
- The payment has not already been processed.
- The transition is permitted.

Do not let a shopkeeper mark another shop's payment as paid.

## Automatic payment workflow

Only implement automatic verification if a real, configured payment provider or supported verified notification mechanism exists.

Validate server-side notification authenticity and the order, amount, currency, transaction reference, and event uniqueness.

Use idempotency and transactions.

Do not implement a fake webhook or simulate a provider's confirmation.

Do not claim automated payment confirmation, settlement, or refunds without a working and tested integration.

## Payment records

Maintain a reliable payment history.

Where supported, record:

- Payment ID.
- Order ID.
- Shop ID.
- Payment method.
- Amount and currency.
- Payment status.
- Customer-provided reference.
- Verified provider reference, where available.
- Verification actor.
- Verification timestamp.
- Failure or rejection reason.
- Refund history.
- Audit events.

Never store payment credentials or UPI PINs.
